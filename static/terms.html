Using BurstAI fairly

Last updated 22 November 2026

The one rule that matters most

A BurstAI score is evidence to look into, never a verdict about a person. Do not penalise, accuse or fail anyone on the basis of a number from this or any other detector alone.

1. What the results mean

Similarity

  • It is the share of counted words that match a source the search could reach: public web pages, Wikipedia, open academic abstracts and your own private document bank.
  • A match is not automatically plagiarism. Properly quoted and cited material, common phrases, titles and the author's own earlier work all match.
  • A low score is not proof of originality. BurstAI cannot see paywalled pages, books, or private archives of student papers, and free search engines sometimes return nothing. The report tells you how many pages were actually compared.

AI-writing likelihood

  • It is a statistical estimate of how predictable the text is to two small open language models, combined with writing-style signals. It is not a detector of any specific product.
  • It works for English only. For other languages no score is shown.
  • It is less reliable on short texts, formulaic genres (lab reports, legal or technical writing) and can unfairly flag people writing in a second language, whose prose tends to be more predictable.
  • It can be wrong in both directions. Text edited after generation, or deliberately disguised, can pass; honest writing can be flagged.
  • The thresholds were calibrated on a small set of samples. Treat the confidence label seriously, and treat "Mixed / uncertain" as exactly that.

Grammar

Suggestions come from a rule-based checker (and from Gemini if you add your own key). Review each one; names, technical terms and deliberate style choices are sometimes flagged.

Signed reports

A signed report proves that the BurstAI engine produced those scores for a text with a particular fingerprint at a particular time, and that nobody altered them afterwards. It does not prove who wrote the text, and it is not a certificate that the work is original.

2. If you are assessing someone else's work

  • Read the matched sources and the flagged sentences yourself before drawing any conclusion.
  • Talk to the writer and give them a real chance to explain: drafts, notes and version history are far better evidence than any score.
  • Follow your institution's academic-integrity procedure. A detector result should never be the only basis for a finding.
  • Make sure you are permitted to submit the person's work to an online service, and tell them you have done so where your rules require it. See the privacy notice for where text is sent.

3. If you are checking your own work

  • Use the source links to add quotation marks and citations where they are missing, or to rewrite in your own words.
  • A clean report does not guarantee that another tool, or your teacher, will see it the same way.
  • Do not use BurstAI to disguise copied or machine-written text as your own. That defeats the purpose of the tool and of your course.

4. Rules for using the service

  • Only scan text you have the right to check, and only upload documents to your document bank that you are allowed to store.
  • Do not try to break, overload or scrape the service, or get around its limits. Fair-use limits (about 30 scans per hour per visitor, roughly 30,000 words per scan) keep it free for everyone.
  • Do not submit unlawful content or other people's sensitive personal data.
  • You are responsible for your account and password. There is currently no email-based password reset.
  • We may remove accounts or block access that abuses the service.
